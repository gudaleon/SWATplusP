# Worked Example 8 — the augmented sorption core at pool level: README v1

Method: Researcher-led AI research.
Status: pool-level implementation and tests complete, 2026-11-19. The build script `build_and_run.py` regenerates every number. The formulation is `SORPTION_CORE_SPEC_v1.md` exactly as frozen before implementation; the control is a line-faithful transcription of the stock routine `nut_pminrl.f90` (sol_p_model = 0) from the audited SWAT+ source, with its equations and constants taken from the source audit (sorption ratio from PSP = 0.4, relaxation factors 0.1 and 0.6, active-to-stable coefficient 0.01 on the four-to-one ratio).

## The result

**All four pre-registered tests separate the augmented core from the stock routine in the direction the specification predicted, and the stock routine fails each one for the structural reason the audit named.** In the pulse test, a fertiliser pulse in the core relaxes in two phases, falling from 30 mg/L to 0.24 mg/L within six hours on the fast exchange and then drifting slowly for a month (0.1021 to 0.1012 mg/L between days 1 and 30) as the ageing transfer proceeds, while the stock labile pool is still relaxing toward its fixed ratio after thirty days (11.2 to 6.9 mg/kg over the month) because its fastest rate is a ten-day constant. In the five-year loading test, the core's sorbed total climbs through 32, 54, 75 and 96 percent of its finite capacity while its solution concentration climbs from 1.3 to 7.1 mg/L, and in the fifth year, with the capacity full, the same daily load has nowhere to go and the solution concentration jumps to 524 mg/L, which is the retention collapse the capacity exists to produce, stated here as a pool-test arithmetic result (a full model would export the excess by runoff and leaching, pathways outside this test); the stock pools under the identical load simply grow, linearly and without limit, to 772.5 mg/kg. In the drawdown test, two states loaded to 50 and 90 percent of capacity equilibrate to solution concentrations of 3.13 and 14.09 mg/L respectively before any uptake begins, so the concentration a plant or a drain sees is governed by how full the soil is, and under a strong sixty-day sink both states release about 7,000 mg/m² from their pools while sustaining final concentrations of 2.40 and 10.26 mg/L; the stock routine has no concentration state at all, and its labile pool under the same sink moves only through its fixed ratio. Phosphorus is conserved to a relative residual of 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ in the core and exactly, by construction, in the stock transcription.

## Repairs made during the build, on the record

Two defects in the first implementation were found by the tests and repaired, and both are recorded in the build script. The explicit exchange step overshot the capacity when the solution concentration far exceeded the equilibrium concentration, producing saturations above 100 percent; the exchange is now an exponential approach to the step equilibrium with the capacity shared between the fast and slow pools, as the specification's loading test words it. The first drawdown test mostly drained the solution store left by fast loading rather than the pools; the test now equilibrates for five days before the sink starts, so it measures the state's own equilibrium concentration and its pool release. Neither repair changed the frozen formulation, its parameters, or the tests' expected behaviours.

## Scope

This example is the pool-level implementation of the augmented core, tested case by case against the stock routine's own mathematics, which is what the branch plan's tests demand. It is not yet the Fortran implementation inside SWAT+: porting the core into the audited source as a switchable branch and running it in a watershed configuration remains the branch's final step, and no watershed-scale claim is made from the pool tests. The mineralization stage (stage 4) and the tile and macropore stage (stage 5) of the ladder are not implemented here; their verified behaviours stand in Examples 6 and 3 respectively, awaiting the same treatment.
