# Worked Example 8 — the Fortran port and the three-branch watershed run: README v1

Method: Researcher-led AI research.
Status: port complete and watershed comparison run, 2026-11-19. The port design and its stated mapping decisions are in `PORT_DESIGN_v1.md`; the pool-level tests of the same core are in `README_v1.md`.

## What was built

**The frozen sorption core now runs inside SWAT+ as a third, switchable phosphorus branch, and in a six-year watershed run it exports soluble phosphorus in surface runoff at five and a half times the default routine's rate and eleven times the alternate routine's, because its exports are priced by an equilibrium concentration that rises with the soil's phosphorus status instead of by a fixed fraction of a pool.** The implementation adds one module (`aug_p_module.f90`, carrying the frozen parameters and the published equilibrium concentrations), one routine (`nut_pminrl3.f90`, which re-splits each layer's labile pool into solution and fast-sorbed parts at the Langmuir equilibrium each day, applies the ageing exchange with the active pool, and leaves the stable pool untouched, as the design note states), a three-line dispatch at the existing `sol_P_model` switch in `hru_control.f90`, and concentration-based export formulas in `nut_solp.f90` fenced by the same switch, so runoff, leaching and tile exports under the augmented branch carry the layer's equilibrium concentration times the water flux. The whole model, 655 source files plus the two new ones, compiles cleanly under gfortran 13.3.0, and all three branches ran the Ames_sub1 configuration (twelve HRUs, window shortened to 1975–1980 and stated here) to successful completion with the same binary, so the controls are the branch's own stock routines and not a different build.

## The comparison, and its limits

Mean annual soluble phosphorus in surface runoff is 0.025 kg/ha under the Jones lineage, 0.012 under Vadas and White, and 0.137 under the augmented core; sediment-bound mineral phosphorus runs the other way, at 0.035, 0.062 and 0.020 kg/ha respectively, because the branches partition the same soil phosphorus differently between the dissolved and sediment pathways. Leached labile phosphorus and tile labile phosphorus are zero in all three branches in this configuration, so the tile comparison the design hoped for is not available from the Ames watershed and no tile claim is made from this run; the tile pathway difference stands on Examples 6 and 3 and on the audit. Three limits are stated plainly. The augmented parameters are the frozen specification's basin-wide constants, not soil-specific values, because the port reads them from the module rather than from per-soil inputs, which the design note allowed and a production version should repair. The default landscape outputs carry fluxes, not soil pool trajectories, so the comparison is made on exports and on stable completion, and the pool-level behaviour of the same mathematics stands on the pool tests. And the difference in exports is a behaviour difference between architectures on one small watershed, not a validation of any branch against field measurements, none of which this example uses.
