# Port design — tile-drainage augmentation for SWAT+ (stage 5)

**Purpose.** The stock SWAT+ tile pathway carries dissolved labile phosphorus only, so particulate phosphorus cannot reach a tile line in the model at any parameter setting. Tile drainage is being installed across the Lake of the Woods basin's agricultural land as standard practice, and the tile-drained field evidence assembled in Worked Example 6 (over half of total phosphorus loss through the tiles in Sadhukhan's manured Ontario field; macroporosity the preeminent sensitivity parameter) says the excluded pathway is the dominant one where tiles and manure coincide. This augmentation adds the missing pathway to the branch model: a surface-connected macropore bypass to tile depth, carrying dissolved phosphorus at surface-layer concentration and particulate phosphorus detached from the first soil layer, alongside the host's existing matrix drainage, which is left unchanged.

## Design decisions

1. **The augmentation is a transport switch, not a seventh pool branch.** Tile transport is orthogonal to the pool formulations of branches 0 to 5, and every one of them deserves to be testable with and without the bypass, giving a formulation-by-transport factorial rather than a single hybrid branch. The switch is therefore file-activated, on the precedent of the nutrients input file: when a control file is present in the simulation folder, the augmentation is on, with its parameters read from that file; when it is absent, tile behaviour is exactly the host's. No basin schema changes are needed.
2. **The macropore flux.** Per HRU and day, the bypass flux is the RZWQM2-P share of the day's infiltrating water, r = Vmac/(Vmac + Vmat), applied to the water the host routes past the surface layer on event days, with r parameterised per soil from its clay content under a stated rule and capped by a stated daily capacity. The host's own crack-flow variable is not used: it routes water into the profile for the soil water balance and is not a surface-to-tile conveyor, and substituting it was already refused in the branch-4 design for the same reason.
3. **Dissolved pathway.** The bypass carries dissolved phosphorus at the surface layer's dissolved concentration of the active pool branch: the published equilibrium concentration of branches 2, 3 and 5, and the host partition concentration of the stock branches and branch 4. The flux is debited from the surface layer's labile pool, and from the tracked dissolved mass under branch 5, through the existing fences.
4. **Particulate pathway.** Detachment from the first layer follows the Jarvis formulation exactly as implemented and verified in Example 6 (equations 129 to 143 of Sadhukhan and Qi 2018), with its sediment store dynamics, its crop factor and its depth attenuation. One stated deviation from RZWQM2-P is forced by the host: Example 6 routes the detached load through a linear groundwater reservoir, but the host's tile flux already leaves a drainage store, so the bypass delivers with the day's tile flow rather than through a second smoothing store.
5. **Reporting.** The bypass's dissolved flux is reported within the tile dissolved total, and its particulate flux within a branch-tracked accumulator written to the branch's own results, so the stock outputs remain comparable across the factorial.

## Parameters and provenance

Detachability, the filter coefficient and the sediment-store parameters carry Example 6's stated assumptions, since the technical report gives no defaults. The macropore share r is the sensitive parameter of the source model and is poorly constrained by standard soil survey data; the design therefore treats it as a stated scenario variable (a low, central and high value per soil class) rather than a quantity to be calibrated, and every result is reported across that range.

## Harness finding (2026-11-19, before the gates)

The tile-enabled variant has been built and its wiring verified: a `tiledrain.str` structures file was authored (one entry, depth 1000 mm, drain time 48 hours, lag 12 hours, radius 25 mm, spacing 20 m, drainage coefficient 25 mm/day, pump capacity 1.042 mm/hr, lateral conductivity factor 1.0 — stated demonstration values), the landuse file's tile column was pointed at it for every landuse, and the model's own checker output registers the tiledrain flag as 1 for the soils. Tile flow nevertheless remains zero, at 1000 mm and again at a shallower 500 mm depth, for a hydrological reason rather than a wiring one: in this test configuration percolation is zero and lateral flow is under a tenth of a millimetre per year against 805 mm of precipitation, because evapotranspiration consumes essentially the whole water balance (water yield 12.8 mm/yr), so no layer ever holds the perched water the tile routine drains. A wetter forcing period was also tested (1988–1998, 942 mm of precipitation per year, percolation 0.92 mm/yr, water yield 26 mm/yr) and tile flow still remained zero, which locates the condition precisely: the host's drain routine (swr_drains) is a Hooghoudt water-table model whose flux is positive only when the computed water table stands within the drain depth of the surface, or under surface ponding, and this landscape's water table sits at depth in the two-metre profile in every tested configuration. A further variant with a restricting layer (saturated conductivity 0.01 mm/hr in the two deep layers) and drains at 700 mm also produced no tile flow, and percolation remained exactly zero in every configuration, which marks the harness itself as evapotranspiration-dominated to a degree that its subsurface pathways never activate. On re-reading, however, the bypass designed below does not need the host's tile flow as its carrier: its macropore flux is computed from the day's infiltrating water and the share parameter, so gates 2 and 3 can be exercised on any configuration with infiltration events, and gate 1 compares the augmented binary with the control file absent against the stock behaviour on the same folder, whatever that behaviour is. A shallow-water-table landscape is therefore not a prerequisite for the bypass build; it remains an open harness task for exercising the host's own tile routine in this configuration, which matters for the Lake of the Woods application rather than for the augmentation. The build proceeds on the infiltration-driven design.

## Amendment (2026-11-19, at build time)

Two decisions above were amended during the build, with the reasons recorded in README_tile_v1.md. First, the macropore flux basis is the day's percolation out of the surface layer, not total infiltration: the infiltration basis, coupled to the stock routine's fast desorption refill, formed a pump, and the percolation basis is the formulation's own. Second, an event threshold of 1 mm of surface-layer percolation defines a bypass day. The build is complete and its three gates pass; the six-by-two factorial over the Lake of the Woods soils is the follow-on run set.

## Gates, stated in advance

The Ames test configuration carries no tile drainage, so the first build task is a tile-enabled variant of it, with the host's drainage parameters set and the stock tile behaviour recorded as the control. Gate one: with the control file absent, the augmented binary reproduces the stock tile outputs exactly. Gate two: with the bypass on, particulate phosphorus appears in tile flow only on days with bypass flux, and is identically zero without it, restating Example 6's structural test in the host. Gate three: the phosphorus ledger of the surface layer closes with the bypass fluxes included. Only after these gates does the factorial comparison (six pool branches, bypass on and off, the four Lake of the Woods soils) run.
