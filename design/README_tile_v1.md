# Tile-drainage bypass — build v1 and gates

**The tile-drainage bypass is built into SWAT+P, and its three pre-registered gates pass: with no control file the augmented model is exactly the stock model, with the control file present particulate phosphorus reaches the tile pathway only on percolation events and is identically zero without them, and every flux the bypass reports is debited from the soil pools in the same code path that reports it.** The bypass is a transport switch independent of the six pool branches, exactly as designed: a control file named tile_bypass.ctl in the simulation folder turns it on, and its absence leaves the model bit-identical in behaviour.

## What was built

On every day when percolation out of the surface layer reaches the stated event threshold of 1 mm, a macropore flux is computed as a share r of that percolation, capped at 10 mm per day. The dissolved pathway carries phosphorus at the surface layer's dissolved concentration: on branches 2, 3 and 5 that is the branch's published equilibrium concentration, and on the stock branches and branch 4 it is the RZWQM2-P leached fraction of the labile pool (Sadhukhan and Qi 2018 equations 119–121, with the retardation denominator in metres of water, exactly as verified in Example 6). The particulate pathway is the Jarvis detachment of Example 6 (equations 129–143) with its sediment-store dynamics, delivering a fraction of the surface layer's total phosphorus — mineral labile, active and stable plus the two humus pools — and deducting the delivered mass from those pools in proportion to their shares, the deduction whose absence in a first draft of Example 6 created phosphorus at exactly the delivery rate. Daily bypass fluxes are written to tile_bypass_out.txt, and the dissolved flux is added to the standard tile dissolved output; the particulate flux is carried in the branch's own accumulator and output file, as designed.

## Gate results

Gate 1 (control): on the tile-enabled test watershed with no control file, the augmented binary reproduces the previous run's outputs exactly — surface soluble, sediment-bound and tile labile phosphorus identical for all twelve HRUs — and creates no bypass output file. Gate 2 (structure): with the control file present at r = 0.3, particulate bypass flux appears on 4,106 HRU-days, each one a day with surface-layer percolation at or above the threshold, and on no other day. Gate 3 (ledger): the bypass output file's dissolved total reconciles with the standard tile output's tile labile phosphorus to within 0.02 kg/ha over the six-year run (the residual is the standard output's area weighting against the file's unweighted sum), and the particulate deductions are taken from the pools in the same statements that accumulate the reported flux, so the surface layer's ledger closes by construction and by the output audit.

## Behaviour, and the coupling finding

On this test watershed the bypass is not a small correction. The watershed's management applies phosphorus fertilizer (including a monoammonium phosphate operation at 142.7 kg of product per hectare), so its labile pool is continually resupplied, and at r = 0.3 the bypass routes 19.6 kg/ha/yr of dissolved and 9.1 kg/ha/yr of particulate phosphorus through the tile pathway, against exactly zero tile phosphorus in the stock configuration. The dissolved flux is only weakly sensitive to r (16.8, 19.6 and 20.2 kg/ha/yr at r = 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5) because the pool fraction and the flux cap dominate it; the particulate flux scales more strongly (4.4, 9.1 and 11.4). On branch 2 the dissolved bypass falls to 1.29 kg/ha/yr, because that branch's equilibrium concentration is low, while its particulate bypass rises to 13.9, because its surface layer carries the larger combined pool the detachment fraction acts on.

Two construction lessons are recorded. First, the flux basis matters more than any parameter: the first build took the macropore flux as a share of total infiltration (runoff plus percolation) and, coupled to the stock routine's fast desorption refill, formed a pump; the percolation basis is both better behaved and the formulation's own, since in RZWQM2-P the macropore flux is a share of the water entering the profile's drainage. Second, the detachment magnitudes run hot on this harness because the formulation was built for a 200 mm surface layer and the test soil's surface layer is 50 mm, which concentrates both the sediment concentration and the stock fraction; the gates test structure, and the magnitudes are reported as harness- and parameter-dependent behaviour, not as field estimates.

## Limits

The host's own tile routine still produces no flow in this harness (the water-table condition documented in the design note), so the bypass's fluxes ride on percolation events rather than on the host's tile hydrograph; on a watershed where the host's tiles flow, the two tile fluxes add. The depth to groundwater in the detachment attenuation is held at the stated 1 m of Example 6. The six-by-two factorial over the Lake of the Woods soils (six pool branches, bypass on and off) is the follow-on run set and has not been run yet.
